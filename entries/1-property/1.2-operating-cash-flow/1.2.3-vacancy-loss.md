## Purpose

Vacancy loss is the rent given up because space is empty. It is the largest
single deduction in most operating statements and the one most often understated
by underwriting a market average rather than the property's own rollover.

## Inputs

Potential Gross Income (1.2.1). The vacancy assumption, or the lease expiration
schedule and expected downtime between tenants.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

Where vacancy is modeled as a rate, multiply potential gross income by that
rate. Where leases are modeled individually, mark each space vacant from lease
expiry through the assumed downtime, and total the rent forgone in each period.

## Rules

1. `Vacancy Loss = Potential Gross Income (1.2.1) x vacancy rate`, or the sum of
   rent forgone on spaces vacant in the period where leases are modeled
   individually.
2. Deducted from Potential Gross Income (1.2.1) as a reduction of revenue.
3. Physical vacancy only. Rent lost to free rent is Concessions (1.2.4) and
   rent billed but not collected is Credit and Collection Loss (1.2.5).
   Economic vacancy is the sum of all three and is not this line.
4. Where leases are modeled individually, a general vacancy allowance may be
   applied on top to cover unscheduled loss. Applying both a full lease-level
   downtime and an unadjusted market vacancy rate double counts.
5. Vacant space forgoes its expense reimbursements (1.2.15) as well as its rent.
