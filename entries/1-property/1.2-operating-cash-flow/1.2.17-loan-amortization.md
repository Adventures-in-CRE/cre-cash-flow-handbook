## Purpose

Amortization is the repayment of loan principal over time. It is the schedule
that splits each debt service payment into interest and principal and carries
the loan balance forward. Every levered figure in a model depends on it,
because the balance it produces is what gets paid off at sale.

## Inputs

Loan Proceeds (1.1.9). The interest rate, the amortization period, the loan
term, and the payment frequency.

## Output

Four periodic streams on the model's period grid: opening balance, interest,
principal, and closing balance.

## Method

Start with the loan amount as the opening balance. Each period, compute
interest on the opening balance. Subtract that interest from the payment to get
principal. Reduce the balance by the principal. Carry the result forward.

## Rules

1. `Interest = opening balance x periodic rate`, where the periodic rate is the
   annual rate divided by the number of periods per year.
2. `Principal = payment - interest`
3. `Closing balance = opening balance - principal`
4. The constant payment is solved over the amortization period, which may be
   longer than the loan term. Where it is longer, a balance remains at maturity
   and is repaid at Loan Payoff (1.3.5).
5. On an interest only loan, principal is zero and the balance is unchanged.
6. A floating rate recomputes interest each period from that period's index
   plus spread. The payment is not held constant.
7. Interest funded from the interest reserve (1.1.12) increases the balance
   rather than reducing it.
