## Purpose

A concession is rent the landlord gives up to get a lease signed. Most often it
is a free rent period. It is what lets an owner hold the face rent on paper
while actually charging less, which is why a rent roll read without concessions
overstates income. Also called an inducement.

## Inputs

The concession terms in each lease, and the periods over which they apply.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

For each lease, identify the concession and the periods it falls in. Where it
is free rent, take the contract rent for those periods. Where it is a waiver of
a charge, take the amount waived. Sum across leases in each period.

## Rules

1. `Concessions = sum over leases of the rent or charges forgone in the period`
2. Deducted from Potential Gross Income (1.2.1) as a reduction of revenue.
3. Recorded in the periods the concession is actually given, not spread across
   the lease term. A model that amortizes concessions states so, and does not
   also record them as given.
4. Free rent at the front of a lease is recorded in those periods at the
   contract rent for the space.
5. Tenant improvement allowances and leasing commissions are capital costs
   (1.2.11), not concessions, even though both are inducements.
6. Feeds Net Effective Rent (1.5.18), which is where the comparison between
   face rent and concession-adjusted rent is made.
