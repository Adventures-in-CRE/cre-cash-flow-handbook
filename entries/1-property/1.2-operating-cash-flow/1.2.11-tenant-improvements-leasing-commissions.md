## Purpose

Signing a tenant costs money. Tenant improvements are the physical work done to
fit the space to that tenant. Leasing commissions are what the brokers are paid
for bringing them. Both are paid at lease signing, and together they are why a
heavily rolling building converts far less of its NOI into cash.

## Inputs

The lease expiration schedule. The renewal probability. Tenant improvement
allowance and commission rate, each stated separately for new and renewing
tenants.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

For each lease expiring in the period, weight the new and renewal cost by the
renewal probability. Apply the improvement allowance per unit of area and the
commission rate against the rent over the new lease term. Sum across leases.

## Rules

1. `Cost per lease = area x allowance rate + total rent over the new term x
   commission rate`, weighted between the new and renewal assumptions by the
   renewal probability.
2. Deducted below Net Operating Income (1.2.9). It never reduces NOI.
3. Recorded in the period the lease commences, unless the entry states an
   amortization treatment.
4. Renewal allowances and commissions are lower than new lease assumptions and
   are stated as separate inputs.
5. Costs committed before the hold period begins are Upfront Tenant
   Improvements and Leasing Commissions (1.1.7).
6. Property types that lease space without tenant-specific buildout carry zero
   on the improvement component and may still carry commissions.
