## Purpose

Capital expenditures replace things that wear out, such as roofs, elevators, and
parking lots. They are lumpy and unavoidable. The reserve is the periodic amount
set aside for them, which turns an irregular cost into a smooth one the model
can carry below net operating income.

## Inputs

The reserve rate, stated per unit, per unit of area, or as a percentage of
effective gross income. Any specific capital project with its own timing.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

Multiply the reserve rate by its basis for the period. Where a specific project
is scheduled, model that cost in the period it is incurred instead of, or on top
of, the reserve.

## Rules

1. `Capital Expenditure Reserve = reserve rate x basis for the period`
2. Deducted below Net Operating Income (1.2.9). It never reduces NOI.
3. A capital expenditure is an expenditure with a useful life greater than one
   year. Costs consumed within the year are operating expenses (1.2.8).
4. Where both a reserve and a scheduled project are modeled, the entry states
   whether the project draws against the accumulated reserve or is additive.
   Recording both without stating this double counts.
5. Work performed before the hold period begins is Upfront Capital Expenditures
   (1.1.6), not this line.
6. Tenant improvements and leasing commissions are capital but are tracked
   separately (1.2.11), because they are driven by leasing rather than by the
   building.
