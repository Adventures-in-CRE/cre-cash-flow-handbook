## Purpose

Potential gross income is what the property would collect if every rentable
space were occupied at market rent and every tenant paid. Nothing about it is
achievable. It is the ceiling the rest of the operating section deducts from,
and stating it explicitly is what makes each deduction visible instead of
buried in a net number.

## Inputs

Rentable area or unit count. Market rent for each space. The rent growth
assumption. The rent roll, where in-place leases are modeled individually.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

For each rentable space, take market rent for the period and multiply by the
area or unit count. Sum across all spaces, occupied and vacant alike. Apply the
growth assumption to move market rent forward across periods.

## Rules

1. `Potential Gross Income = sum over all rentable spaces of (market rent x
   area or units)`
2. Stated at 100 percent occupancy. Vacant space is included at market rent.
3. Stated at market rent, not contract rent. The gap between the two is Loss to
   Lease (1.2.2).
4. Area is rentable area, which is a tenant's usable area plus its share of
   common area.
5. Trended models grow market rent by the stated rate each period. Untrended
   models hold it flat. The entry states which is used.
6. Excludes parking, storage, and fee revenue, which are Other Income (1.2.6),
   and expense recoveries, which are Expense Reimbursements (1.2.15).
