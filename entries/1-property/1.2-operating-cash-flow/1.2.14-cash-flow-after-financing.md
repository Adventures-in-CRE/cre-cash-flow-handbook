## Purpose

Cash flow after financing is the cash available to equity for a single
operating period once debt has been serviced. It is the levered counterpart
to Cash Flow from Operations (1.2.12), and the base from which Cash-on-Cash
Return and Net Levered Cash Flow are built.

## Inputs

Cash Flow from Operations (1.2.12) for the period. Debt Service (1.2.13) for
the same period.

## Output

One figure per operating period, in the model's currency, before any
terminal-year sale adjustment.

## Method

Subtract debt service from cash flow from operations, for each period
independently.

## Rules

1. `CFAF = Cash Flow from Operations (1.2.12) - Debt Service (1.2.13)`,
   computed independently for every operating period.
2. Debt Service means total scheduled debt service, interest plus principal
   amortization, not accrual interest expense alone.
3. Excludes reversion proceeds and loan payoff at sale. Those enter only in
   the terminal period, at Net Levered Cash Flow (1.4.2).
4. This entry varies on leverage: it exists only where leverage is levered.
   Under unlevered leverage there is no separate line; use Cash Flow from
   Operations (1.2.12) directly.
5. Computed after, and separately from, Operating Expenses (1.2.8) and Net
   Operating Income (1.2.9); it never nets against either.

## Worked example

A single period on an annual grid, continuing the example set in 1.2.9 and
1.2.12.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cash Flow from Operations (1.2.12) | 555,000 |
| Debt Service (1.2.13) | (310,000) |
| **Cash Flow after Financing** | **245,000** |
