## Purpose

Leases are signed at a point in time and the market moves after that. Loss to
lease is the gap between what in-place leases actually charge and what the same
space would rent for today. It tells an owner how much rent is recoverable as
leases roll, which is the whole thesis of most value-add multifamily deals.

## Inputs

Potential Gross Income (1.2.1) at market rent. Contract rent on in-place
leases.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

For each occupied space, subtract the contract rent from the market rent for
the same space in the same period. Sum across occupied spaces. The result is
deducted from potential gross income.

## Rules

1. `Loss to Lease = sum over occupied spaces of (market rent - contract rent)`
2. Computed on occupied space only. Vacant space carries no lease and
   contributes nothing here. It is handled by Vacancy Loss (1.2.3).
3. Deducted from Potential Gross Income (1.2.1) as a reduction of revenue.
4. Where contract rent exceeds market rent, the figure is negative and is
   reported as gain to lease rather than netted silently to zero.
5. Resets when a lease rolls. From the renewal or new lease date, contract rent
   becomes the newly signed rent.
6. Free rent and other inducements are Concessions (1.2.4), not this line.
