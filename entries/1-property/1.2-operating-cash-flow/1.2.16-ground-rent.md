## Purpose

Under a ground lease one party owns the land and another owns the buildings on
it. Ground rent is what the owner of the buildings pays for the right to occupy
the land. It runs for decades, it is senior to the mortgage in most cases, and
it is the reason a leasehold interest is worth less than a fee simple one.

## Inputs

The ground lease rent schedule, its escalation terms, and any participation in
property revenue.

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

Read the scheduled rent for the period from the lease. Apply the escalation the
lease specifies. Where the lease takes a share of property revenue, compute
that share and add it.

## Rules

1. Ground rent is an operating expense and feeds Operating Expenses (1.2.8). It
   reduces Net Operating Income (1.2.9).
2. Escalations follow the lease, whether fixed, indexed, or reset by appraisal
   at stated dates.
3. Where the lease takes a percentage of revenue, that share is computed on the
   revenue base the lease names, not on Effective Gross Income (1.2.7) by
   default.
4. A model of a leasehold interest values the improvements only. Gross Sale
   Price (1.3.2) is the leasehold value, and the exit cap rate (1.5.12) applied
   to it reflects the remaining lease term.
5. A model of the fee interest treats the same payment as revenue, not expense.
   One property never records both.
