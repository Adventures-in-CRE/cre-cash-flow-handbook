## Purpose

Cash flow from operations is what the property generates after capital costs
tied to keeping and re-leasing space, but before debt service. Net Operating
Income (1.2.9) measures earning power. This line measures what is actually
left, once the property has paid to maintain and re-lease itself, to either
service debt or return to the owner.

## Inputs

Net Operating Income (1.2.9). Capital Expenditure Reserves (1.2.10). Tenant
Improvements & Leasing Commissions (1.2.11).

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

Subtract capital expenditure reserves and tenant improvements and leasing
commissions from net operating income.

## Rules

1. `Cash Flow from Operations = Net Operating Income (1.2.9) - Capital
   Expenditure Reserves (1.2.10) - Tenant Improvements & Leasing Commissions
   (1.2.11)`
2. Computed once per period, on the model's period grid.
3. Excludes debt service (1.2.13), income tax, depreciation, and
   amortization. Those sit below this line, starting at Cash Flow after
   Financing (1.2.14).
4. Reserve deposits are subtracted in the period they are funded, not the
   period the capital is spent.
5. A period with no leasing activity and no reserve deposit has this line
   equal to NOI for that period.
6. Does not vary by leverage. Unlevered and levered models compute this line
   identically; leverage enters at Debt Service (1.2.13).

## Worked example

A single period on an annual grid, continuing the example set in 1.2.9.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Net Operating Income (1.2.9) | 620,000 |
| Capital Expenditure Reserves (1.2.10) | (25,000) |
| Tenant Improvements & Leasing Commissions (1.2.11) | (40,000) |
| **Cash Flow from Operations** | **555,000** |
