## Purpose

Total investment cost is everything spent to acquire and ready the property
before it earns stabilized income: the price, closing costs, any build costs,
upfront capital work, reserves funded at closing, and capitalized financing
costs. Some models call it the all-in basis. It is the uses side of sources
and uses (1.1.14) and the base for equity required (1.1.11).

## Inputs

Acquisition Cost (1.1.1). Closing & Due Diligence Costs (1.1.2). Hard Costs
(1.1.3). Soft Costs (1.1.4). Contingency (1.1.5). Upfront Capital
Expenditures (1.1.6). Upfront Tenant Improvements & Leasing Commissions
(1.1.7). Interest Reserve (1.1.12). Lender fees and other capitalized
financing costs.

## Output

One figure for the deal, and optionally one per period.

## Method

Add every use of funds made before stabilized operations begin.

## Rules

1. `Total Investment Cost = sum of the Inputs`
2. Lines that do not apply are zero. An acquisition normally uses the price,
   closing costs, lender fees and upfront capital work. A development adds
   hard costs, soft costs, contingency and the interest reserve.
3. Lender fees are paid at closing and are a use. They are not netted from
   Loan Proceeds (1.1.9), so the loan stays at its gross amount.
4. Excludes costs after acquisition: Debt Service (1.2.13), reserve deposits
   (1.2.10), and selling costs (1.3.3).
5. Equals Total Uses in Sources and Uses (1.1.14).

## Worked example

An acquisition with no development lines. Lender fees are 1% of a loan of
4,875,000, which is 65% of the price.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Acquisition Cost (1.1.1) | 7,500,000 |
| Closing & Due Diligence Costs (1.1.2) | 75,000 |
| Lender fees | 48,750 |
| Upfront Capital Expenditures (1.1.6) | 100,000 |
| **Total Investment Cost** | **7,723,750** |
