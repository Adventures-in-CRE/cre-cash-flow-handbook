## Purpose

Acquisition cost is the price the buyer pays the seller for the property, or
for the land of a development site, before any other cost of the deal. It is
the starting figure of the investment, the base on which closing costs (1.1.2)
are usually measured, and the denominator of the going-in cap rate (1.5.11).

## Inputs

The contract purchase price. Where the price is solved rather than entered,
the target it is solved from.

## Output

One figure, at the acquisition date, in the model's currency.

## Method

Take the agreed purchase price. Where no price is agreed, solve one from a
target, then state the result as a single price.

## Rules

1. `Acquisition Cost = contract purchase price`
2. Despite the name, this is the price only. Closing and due diligence costs
   (1.1.2), lender fees, and capital expenditures are separate costs on top.
   Practitioners sometimes call those "acquisition costs"; here they are not.
3. A model may enter the price or solve it. Common solves: the present value
   of the cash flows at a target discount rate, stabilized net operating
   income divided by a cap rate, and replacement cost. For a site, residual
   land value (1.5.20) caps the price; it does not set it.
4. Paid at the acquisition date, before the first operating period.

## Worked example

A single acquisition with an entered price.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Contract purchase price | 7,500,000 |
| **Acquisition Cost** | **7,500,000** |
