## Purpose

Contingency is money set aside for costs that are not yet known. It is not
padding and it is not profit. It is the budget's acknowledgment that some line
will come in over, and it is drawn down as those overruns are identified.

## Inputs

Hard Costs (1.1.3). Soft Costs (1.1.4). The contingency rate.

## Output

One figure for the project, and a periodic draw against that balance.

## Method

Apply the contingency rate to the cost base it covers. Draw against the balance
as overruns are identified, reducing the amount remaining.

## Rules

1. `Contingency = contingency rate x the cost base it covers`
2. The entry states which base applies. A hard cost contingency covers Hard
   Costs (1.1.3) only. A project contingency covers hard and soft costs
   together.
3. Drawn down as overruns are recognized. The undrawn balance stays in the
   budget and remains a use of funds until it is released.
4. Contingency released at completion reduces Total Investment Cost (1.1.8) and
   increases the equity returned or the loan repaid.
5. Excludes the interest reserve (1.1.12), which covers a known cost rather than
   an unknown one.
6. Feeds Total Investment Cost (1.1.8).
