## Purpose

Loan payoff is the outstanding loan principal that is repaid to the lender out
of the sale proceeds at exit. It is the balance still owed after the last
scheduled payment of the hold, taken from the amortization schedule (1.2.17).
It is subtracted from sale proceeds to find the cash that reaches equity
(1.3.6).

## Inputs

Loan balance at the end of the exit period, from Loan Amortization (1.2.17).

## Output

One figure per exit date, in the model's currency.

## Method

Take the loan balance at the end of the exit period.

## Rules

1. `Loan Payoff = Loan Balance (1.2.17) at the end of the exit period`
2. The balance is measured after the exit period's scheduled payment. That
   payment is already in Debt Service (1.2.13) and is not repaid twice.
3. During an interest-only period the balance equals the original principal.
   After it, the balance falls with each principal payment.
4. Computed once per candidate exit date.
5. Excludes prepayment penalties and exit fees. A model that charges them
   adds them as a separate cost, not into the balance.
6. Varies on leverage. With no loan the payoff is zero, and the reversion
   figure equals net sale proceeds (1.3.4).

## Worked example

A 10-year hold on an annual grid, continuing the example in 1.3.2. The
balance below is illustrative and comes from the loan's amortization schedule.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Loan Balance at end of year 10 (1.2.17) | 4,350,000 |
