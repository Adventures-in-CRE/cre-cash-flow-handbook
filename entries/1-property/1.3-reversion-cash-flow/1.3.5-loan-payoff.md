## Purpose

Loan payoff is the amount repaid to the lender when the loan ends, normally
out of the sale proceeds at exit. It is the balance still owed after the last
scheduled payment, taken from the amortization schedule (1.2.17), plus any
prepayment charge. It is subtracted from sale proceeds to find the cash that
reaches equity (1.3.6).

## Inputs

Loan balance at the payoff date, from Loan Amortization (1.2.17). Prepayment
penalty, if any.

## Output

One figure per payoff date, in the model's currency.

## Method

Take the loan balance at the payoff date and add any prepayment penalty.

## Rules

1. `Loan Payoff = Loan Balance (1.2.17) at the payoff date + Prepayment
   Penalty`
2. The payoff date is the earlier of the exit date and loan maturity. A loan
   that matures before the sale is paid off at maturity.
3. The balance is measured after that period's scheduled payment, which is
   already in Debt Service (1.2.13) and is not repaid twice.
4. The balance is taken as 1.2.17 reports it. In an interest-only period it
   equals the original principal, plus any interest funded from an interest
   reserve.
5. Prepayment Penalty is zero where the lender charges none, and is shown
   apart from the balance.
6. Computed once per candidate exit date.
7. Varies on leverage. With no loan the payoff is zero, and the reversion
   figure equals Net Reversion Proceeds (1.3.4).

## Worked example

A 10-year hold on an annual grid, continuing the example in 1.3.2. The
balance below is illustrative and comes from the loan's amortization schedule.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Loan Balance at end of year 10 (1.2.17) | 4,350,000 |
| Prepayment Penalty | 0 |
| **Loan Payoff** | **4,350,000** |
