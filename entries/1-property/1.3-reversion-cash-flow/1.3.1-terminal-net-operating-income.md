## Purpose

Terminal net operating income is the income figure a buyer capitalizes to
price the property at sale. A buyer pays for the income they will receive, not
the income the seller just earned, so this line takes the net operating income
of the period after the exit date. It is the numerator of the sale price
(1.3.2).

## Inputs

Net Operating Income (1.2.9) for each period, including one period beyond the
exit date. Hold period, in periods.

## Output

One figure, in the model's currency, for the exit date.

## Method

Take the net operating income of the first period after the exit date.

## Rules

1. `Terminal NOI = Net Operating Income (1.2.9) of period (exit period + 1)`
2. The exit period is the last period of the hold. A sale at the end of year
   10 uses year 11 net operating income.
3. The projection must therefore run one period past the hold. If it does
   not, the model cannot price a sale at its last period.
4. Uses the same net operating income definition as the hold-period figures.
   Capital reserves, leasing costs, and debt service are not deducted
   (1.2.12 and 1.2.13 sit below it).
5. When a model prices a sale at any period in the hold, not only the last,
   it recomputes this figure for each candidate exit period.
6. Does not vary by property type or leverage.

## Worked example

A 10-year hold on an annual grid, continuing the example set in 1.2.9, where
NOI is 620,000 in the exit year.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Net Operating Income, year 10 (exit year) | 620,000 |
| Net Operating Income, year 11 | 638,600 |
| **Terminal Net Operating Income** | **638,600** |
