## Purpose

Loan to value is the outstanding loan balance divided by the value of the
property. It is the lender's measure of how much room sits between what is owed
and what the asset would fetch. It moves with the market even when the loan does
not, which is why a loan that was safe at origination can breach it later.

## Inputs

Loan Amortization (1.2.17), for the outstanding balance. The property's value at
the measurement date.

## Output

A rate, stated as a percentage, at a point in time.

## Method

Take the outstanding loan balance at the measurement date. Divide it by the
property's value at that same date.

## Rules

1. `Loan to Value = outstanding loan balance / property value`
2. The balance comes from Loan Amortization (1.2.17) at the measurement date,
   not the original commitment.
3. Value is the appraised or market value at that date. At acquisition it is
   Acquisition Cost (1.1.1) where price and value agree. At disposition it is
   Gross Sale Price (1.3.2).
4. Stated at a point in time, not per period.
5. Where mezzanine debt (1.1.15) is present, it is excluded from the senior
   ratio and included in a separately stated total.
6. Cost is not value. The ratio measured against cost is Loan-to-Cost (1.5.10).
