## Purpose

Development spread is the difference between yield-on-cost and the market cap
rate for the finished property. It measures what the developer is paid for
taking construction and lease-up risk. An investor could buy a stabilized
building instead of building one, so if the spread is thin there is no reason
to build.

## Inputs

Yield-on-Cost (1.5.15). Exit Cap Rate (1.5.12).

## Output

A rate, stated in basis points, for the project as a whole.

## Method

Subtract the market cap rate for the stabilized property from the yield-on-cost.
Express the difference in basis points.

## Rules

1. `Development Spread = Yield-on-Cost (1.5.15) - market cap rate`
2. The market cap rate is Exit Cap Rate (1.5.12) where the model assumes a sale
   at stabilization. Where the model assumes a hold, it is the cap rate a buyer
   would pay at the stabilization date.
3. Stated in basis points, where one basis point is 0.01 percent.
4. Both terms are computed on the same basis. A trended yield-on-cost is
   compared against a cap rate at the stabilization date, and an untrended one
   against a cap rate today.
5. A negative spread means the finished property is worth less than it cost to
   build.
