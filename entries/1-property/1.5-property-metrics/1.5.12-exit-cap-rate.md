## Purpose

The exit cap rate is the rate a future buyer is assumed to pay, applied to
terminal net operating income to produce the sale price. It is the most
consequential assumption in most models, because it sets a number that nothing
inside the model can check.

## Inputs

The assumed market cap rate at the disposition date.

## Output

A rate, stated as a percentage, at the disposition date.

## Method

Set the rate from the going-in rate, adjusted for the property's age, its
remaining lease term at exit, and the expected market at that date. Apply it to
terminal net operating income to produce the gross sale price.

## Rules

1. Stated as a percentage and applied at the disposition date.
2. `Gross Sale Price (1.3.2) = Terminal Net Operating Income (1.3.1) / Exit Cap
   Rate`
3. This is an assumption, not a computed result. Nothing in the model derives it.
4. Terminal net operating income is the twelve months following the sale date,
   not the twelve months preceding it, because that is what the buyer is buying.
5. Where the exit rate is set equal to the going-in rate (1.5.11), the entry
   records that as a deliberate assumption rather than leaving it implied.
6. Feeds Development Spread (1.5.16), where it stands for the market cap rate on
   the finished property.
