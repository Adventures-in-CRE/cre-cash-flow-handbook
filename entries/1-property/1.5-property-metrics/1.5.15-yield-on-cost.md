## Purpose

Yield-on-cost is stabilized net operating income divided by total project cost.
It is the return a developer earns on what the project actually costs to build,
as opposed to a cap rate, which is a return on what the finished property is
worth. Comparing the two is how a development is judged worth doing. It is also
called development yield.

## Inputs

Net Operating Income (1.2.9) at stabilization. Total Investment Cost (1.1.8).

## Output

A rate, stated as a percentage, for the project as a whole.

## Method

Take net operating income in the first stabilized period. Divide it by total
project cost including land, hard costs, soft costs, contingency, and financing
costs. State whether the numerator is trended or untrended.

## Rules

1. `Yield-on-Cost = stabilized Net Operating Income (1.2.9) / Total Investment
   Cost (1.1.8)`
2. Stabilized means the first period in which the property reaches its assumed
   stabilized occupancy and stays there.
3. Where the numerator is annualized from a shorter period, it is multiplied by
   the number of periods per year without growth applied.
4. A trended yield uses net operating income grown to the stabilization date. An
   untrended yield uses net operating income in today's dollars. The entry
   states which is used. The two are not compared to each other.
5. Total Investment Cost includes the interest reserve (1.1.12) and all
   capitalized financing costs.
