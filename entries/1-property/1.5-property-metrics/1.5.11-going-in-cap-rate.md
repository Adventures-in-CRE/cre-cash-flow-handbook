## Purpose

The going-in cap rate is first year net operating income divided by the purchase
price. It is how a buyer states what they paid in terms every other buyer
understands. It is a price expressed as a yield, which is why it travels between
deals better than a price does.

## Inputs

Net Operating Income (1.2.9) for the first year of the hold. Acquisition Cost
(1.1.1).

## Output

A rate, stated as a percentage, at acquisition.

## Method

Take net operating income for the first twelve months of ownership. Divide by
the purchase price.

## Rules

1. `Going-In Cap Rate = first year Net Operating Income (1.2.9) / Acquisition
   Cost (1.1.1)`
2. The numerator is the first twelve months of ownership. It is not the trailing
   twelve months and it is not a stabilized year.
3. The denominator is the purchase price. Closing and due diligence costs
   (1.1.2) are excluded unless the entry states an all-in basis.
4. The numerator is before capital expenditure reserves (1.2.10) and before
   tenant improvements and leasing commissions (1.2.11).
5. A property that is not stabilized at acquisition produces a going-in rate
   below its stabilized yield. The stabilized measure against cost is
   Yield-on-Cost (1.5.15).
