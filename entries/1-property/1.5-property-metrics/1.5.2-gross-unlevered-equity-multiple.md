## Purpose

The equity multiple is total cash returned divided by total cash invested. It
ignores when the money arrived, which is exactly why it is read next to IRR. A
2.00x over three years and a 2.00x over ten are the same multiple and very
different deals.

## Inputs

Net Unlevered Cash Flow (1.4.1).

## Output

A multiple, stated to two decimal places, for the holding period as a whole.

## Method

Sum every positive cash flow in the stream. Sum every negative cash flow.
Divide the first by the absolute value of the second.

## Rules

1. `Equity Multiple = sum of positive cash flows / absolute value of the sum of
   negative cash flows`
2. Computed on Net Unlevered Cash Flow (1.4.1) over the full holding period,
   including reversion.
3. Stated as a multiple, not a percentage. A multiple of 1.00 means capital came
   back and nothing more.
4. Timing does not affect the result. Two streams with the same totals on
   different dates produce the same multiple.
5. Excludes debt. The levered figure is Gross Levered Equity Multiple (1.5.5).
6. Also called EMx, MOIC, and multiple on invested capital.
