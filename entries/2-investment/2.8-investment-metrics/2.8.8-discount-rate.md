## Purpose

The discount rate converts future cash flows into what they are worth today. It
is the return an investor requires for taking on the risk and the timing of a
given stream of cash. A higher rate means the investor thinks the cash is less
certain, and it produces a lower present value.

## Inputs

The investor's required return for the risk of the cash flow stream being
discounted.

## Output

A rate, stated as a percentage per year, applied across the analysis.

## Method

Set the rate to the return required for cash flows of this risk. Convert it to
the model's period frequency before applying it. Apply it to the cash flow
stream to produce a present value.

## Rules

1. Stated as an annual nominal rate, and converted to the period rate by
   dividing by the number of periods per year unless the entry states that
   compounding is used.
2. The rate discounts a cash flow stream of matching leverage. An unlevered
   stream is discounted at an unlevered rate and a levered stream at a levered
   rate. Mixing the two is an error.
3. The rate discounts nominal cash flows where the model grows income, and real
   cash flows where it does not.
4. Feeds Net Present Value (2.8.6).
5. The discount rate is an assumption, not a computed result. Where it equals
   the internal rate of return (2.8.1), net present value is zero.
