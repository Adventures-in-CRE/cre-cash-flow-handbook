## Purpose

Compound annual growth rate states the constant annual rate at which an
investment would have to grow to turn the money put in into the money taken
out. It answers a narrower question than IRR, because it ignores when the
interim cash flows arrived. That makes it useful where those timings are
irregular or unknown. It is also called CAGR and the geometric mean.

## Inputs

Unlevered Equity Multiple (2.8.2) or the equivalent levered multiple. The
holding period in years.

## Output

A rate, stated as a percentage per year, for the investment as a whole.

## Method

Take the equity multiple, which is total distributions divided by total
contributions. Raise it to the power of one divided by the number of years
held. Subtract one.

## Rules

1. `CAGR = Equity Multiple ^ (1 / n) - 1`, where n is the holding period in
   years.
2. n is measured from the first contribution to the final distribution, in
   years, including fractions.
3. The multiple used and the resulting rate share the same leverage. An
   unlevered multiple produces an unlevered rate.
4. Interim cash flow timing does not affect the result. Where timing matters,
   IRR (2.8.1) is the correct metric.
5. Where the equity multiple is below 1.0, the result is negative.
