## Purpose

Distributable cash is the cash the partnership has in a period to split between
the partners. It is the deal's net levered cash flow after sponsor fees, and the
figure every waterfall tier draws on.

## Inputs

Net Levered Cash Flow (1.4.2). Sponsor fees (3.15.1 to 3.15.4).

## Output

One figure per period, in the model's currency, on the model's period grid.

## Method

In each period, take net levered cash flow and subtract the sponsor fees paid
in that period.

## Rules

1. `Distributable Cash = Net Levered Cash Flow (1.4.2) - Sponsor Fees (3.15.1 to 3.15.4)`,
   per period
2. Fees come out before the waterfall. The tiers split what is left.
3. In the exit period it includes the reversion proceeds, because Net Levered
   Cash Flow does.
4. It can be negative. A negative period is a shortfall, funded by capital
   calls (3.9.1, 3.9.4), not a distribution.
5. Each fee is counted once. Here it reduces the cash. In GP Net Cash Flow
   (3.13.10) it is added to the GP.
6. Before tax.
7. Where no fees are charged it equals Net Levered Cash Flow.

## Worked example

Example, in whole dollars. No sponsor fees are charged. Years 4 to 9 are
omitted.

| Period | Net levered cash flow | Fees | **Distributable Cash** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closing | (2,657,125) | 0 | **(2,657,125)** |
| Year 1 | (281,825) | 0 | **(281,825)** |
| Year 2 | (75,643) | 0 | **(75,643)** |
| Year 3 | 191,399 | 0 | **191,399** |
| Year 10 | 6,003,534 | 0 | **6,003,534** |

Year 10 is the operating cash flow after financing, 214,264, plus the net cash
on sale, 5,789,269.
