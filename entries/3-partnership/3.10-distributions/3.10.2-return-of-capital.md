## Purpose

Return of capital is the part of a distribution that pays a partner back the
capital it contributed, as opposed to a return on that capital. It reduces
unreturned capital. When unreturned capital reaches zero, the partner has its
money back.

## Inputs

Contributions (3.9.2, 3.9.3). Each partner's share of Distributable Cash
(3.10.1). Preferred return owed (3.10.3).

## Output

One figure per partner per period, in the model's currency.

## Method

From the partner's share of the cash, pay the preferred return owed first.
What is left, up to unreturned capital, is return of capital.

## Rules

1. `Return of Capital = MIN(Unreturned Capital, MAX(Share of Distributable Cash - Preferred Return Owed, 0))`,
   per partner
2. Preferred return owed is this period's accrual plus any unpaid from earlier
   periods (3.10.3). It is paid before any capital is returned.
3. Unreturned Capital is cumulative contributions less cumulative Return of
   Capital, at the start of the period.
4. It is zero in any period where the share of cash does not clear the
   preferred return owed.
5. LP and GP capital are returned on the same terms, each in proportion to its
   share of the first tier (3.9.3).
6. Total Return of Capital over the hold cannot exceed total contributions.
7. Cash beyond this tier goes to the later tiers (3.10.4 to 3.10.6).

## Worked example

LP, from the A.CRE Apartment Acquisition Model v2.5, Investor Returns, rounded to
whole dollars. LP contributions total 2,713,134, all called by Year 2. Years 4
to 9 are omitted. They are the same as Year 3: the cash does not clear the
preferred return owed.

| Period | LP share of cash | Unreturned capital | Preferred return owed | **Return of Capital** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 3 | 172,259 | 2,713,134 | 668,731 | **0** |
| Year 10 | 5,403,180 | 2,713,134 | 1,345,581 | **2,713,134** |

In Year 10 the LP receives 1,345,581 of preferred return, then its full
2,713,134 of capital: 4,058,715, the model's first-tier LP distribution.
