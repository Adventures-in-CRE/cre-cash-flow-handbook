## Purpose

The capital call schedule says how much equity is called in each period. It is
the per-period amount the partners' contributions are split from. Over the hold
it adds up to the Total Equity Requirement (3.9.1).

## Inputs

Equity Required at Acquisition (1.1.11). Distributable Cash (3.10.1).

## Output

One figure per period, the total across all partners, in the model's currency.

## Method

Call the equity required at closing. In each later period, call the shortfall,
if any.

## Rules

1. At closing, `Capital Call = Equity Required at Acquisition (1.1.11)`. In each
   later period, `Capital Call = -Distributable Cash (3.10.1)` where that is
   negative, otherwise zero.
2. This is the total before the partner split. Each partner's call is Capital
   Call x that partner's share (3.9.2, 3.9.3).
3. A shortfall is called in the period it arises. No cash is called ahead of
   need.
4. The calls add up to the Total Equity Requirement (3.9.1).
5. Gross. A distribution in one period does not reduce a call in another.
6. A period holds a call or a distribution, never both, because Distributable
   Cash is one figure.
7. A commitment is not a call. Only called amounts are contributions.

## Worked example

Example, in whole dollars, with a 90% LP share and a 10% GP share. Years 3 to 10
need no calls.

| Period | Capital Call | LP (90%) | GP (10%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closing | 2,657,125 | 2,391,413 | 265,713 |
| Year 1 | 281,825 | 253,643 | 28,183 |
| Year 2 | 75,643 | 68,079 | 7,564 |
| **Total** | **3,014,593** | **2,713,134** | **301,459** |

Totals come from unrounded figures, so a column can differ from the sum of its
rounded rows by 1.
