# Definitions

Terms used across the handbook, defined once. Entries specify line items. This
page defines the vocabulary those entries are written in.

Terms are listed alphabetically. Where the [A.CRE Glossary of Real Estate
Terms](https://www.adventuresincre.com/glossary/) defines a term, this page
uses that definition and says so. Where it does not, the definition is the
handbook's own and is marked as such.

## Investment-Level Cash Flow

The cash flow of the investment, where the investment is one or more
properties, or one or more uses within a single property, underwritten as one
thing. It is built by rolling up the property-level and use-level cash flows
beneath it, plus any debt held across the investment rather than against a
single property.

Returns measured here are gross returns. Nothing the sponsor earns appears at
this level: no acquisition, asset management, development, or disposition fee,
and no promote. This level answers what the real estate produces, independent
of who runs it and what they charge for doing so.

Where the investment is a single property with a single use, the roll-up is
trivial and this equals the property-level cash flow.

Also called portfolio-level cash flow where the investment holds more than one
property.

Region 2. Not to be confused with Investment Cash Flow, section 1.1, which is
the capital into and out of a single property.

*Not in the A.CRE Glossary. Specific to this handbook.*

## Partnership-Level Cash Flow

Every investment held by the partnership, rolled up, then reduced by the fees
the sponsor earns and divided among the partners under the partnership
agreement. A partnership can hold more than one investment.

It runs in two directions: contributions, which are what each partner funds and
when, and distributions, which are what each partner receives and in what
priority. The priority rules are the waterfall: return of capital, preferred
return, catch-up, promote, and residual split.

Everything the sponsor earns is modeled here and nowhere else. Acquisition,
asset management, development, and disposition fees reduce the cash available
to distribute. The promote reallocates distributed cash from the limited
partner to the general partner.

Returns measured here are net returns. The gap between gross and net is exactly
the sponsor's economics, which is what lets a limited partner weigh one
sponsor's fee and promote structure against another's.

## Property-Level Cash Flow

The cash flow of a single real estate asset, standing alone. It covers what the
property costs to acquire or build, what it earns while it is held, and what it
returns when it is sold. Who owns the asset, and how that ownership is divided,
does not appear here.

Where a property has more than one use, each use can carry its own cash flow,
returns, and value. So this level supports two grains: one per property, and
one per use within it. Which grain a model uses is a modeling choice, not a
change in definition.

Debt secured by the individual property appears here, which is why this level
produces both an unlevered and a levered cash flow. Debt held across the
investment belongs above.
